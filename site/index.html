Weaving thin-film systems that perform.

Navette is a scattering-matrix engine for light in layered media. It stays stable however thick or absorbing a layer is, treats a millimetre substrate as incoherent where that is the physics, and designs coatings with the needle method on the same engine it simulates with.

01 · Physics

Stable by construction, rigorous by default

Each figure below is a direct engine run. The script that produced them, site/scripts/build_data.py, regenerates this page's data from the installed package.

No overflow, at any thickness

The textbook characteristic matrix multiplies cos δ and sin δ of a complex phase. Through an absorbing layer these grow like eIm δ, and past Im δ ≈ 709 a 64-bit float overflows.

Here a 525 µm silicon wafer sits in the stack as an ordinary layer. The naive transfer matrix returns NaN below 474 nm, where silicon absorbs strongly. Navette combines layers with the Redheffer star product, whose entries are bounded reflection and transmission amplitudes. It returns the physical answer everywhere: the front-surface Fresnel reflectance where the wafer is opaque, and the incoherent two-surface sum where it starts to transmit. Past 1000 nm the coherent naive answer is correct but useless: its fringes are about 0.3 nm apart on a 2 nm grid, so it samples them at random.

  • Redheffer star product, no exponentially growing terms
  • Incoherent wafer: intensity star product across the flagged layer

The whole angle–wavelength plane in one pass

Operations run over the full (λ × θ) grid at once, in parallel across all cores with rayon. This map is 201 wavelengths by 35 angles of a 16-film TiO₂/SiO₂ mirror on BK7. The bright band is the stopband. It shifts toward the blue as the angle grows and, for p-polarisation, narrows towards the Brewster angle.

  • Full s and p treatment, Azzam & Bashara conventions
  • BK7 substrate from its Sellmeier dispersion

Coherence where it is real

Under a TiO₂ film, a 1 mm glass slide treated as fully coherent rings with fringes 0.12 nm apart. No spectrometer resolves them, and no real source is coherent over that path. Flag the slide incoherent and Navette splits the stack into coherent blocks, using a complex star product inside each block and an intensity star product across them (Katsidis & Siapkas 2002). The flagged answer is exactly the coherent one averaged over a round-trip phase period.

  • Three coherence modes, one of which refuses a cross-channel answer it cannot give
  • Checked against closed forms, not only against itself

02 · Design

From a poor guess to a 0.06 % coating

Navette runs Tikhonravov's needle method natively. The optimizer probes every depth in the stack with an infinitesimal layer of the other material, inserts one where the merit gradient is most negative, and re-optimises all thicknesses with bounded Levenberg–Marquardt.

Joint targets

Spectral, angular and CIE colour demands in one merit, with analytic chain-rule terms in the needle gradient.

Several surroundings

A design segment defined once and optimised against every environment, for example bare and laminated, at the same time.

Graded media

Mixture gradients are a layer property. A graded span's thickness is one parameter, not one per sublayer.

Tolerances as a model

Six error channels × four laws, for Monte-Carlo runs of what the coater will actually deposit.

Graded versus stepped

Eight periods at 600 nm, built two ways. As alternating linear FixedSpan gradients (a triangular rugate) and as quarter-wave pairs. The stepped stack has the wider stopband and the stronger sidelobes. Below the band its ripple averages 30 %, against 5 % for the graded profile. Above the band both ring: a triangle is one Fourier component away from a true sinusoidal rugate, which would suppress that side too.

The cost is real and shown, not hidden: 80 solver rows against 16, because the solver sees each span's sublayers.

mirror.py
import numpy as np
from navette.smatrix.smatrix import Request, ScatterMatrix

wl = np.linspace(380.0, 780.0, 401)            # nm
H, L, lam0 = 2.35, 1.46, 560.0                 # TiO2 / SiO2, design wavelength

n = [1.0] + [H, L] * 8 + [1.52]                # ambient, 16 films, glass
d = [0.0] + [lam0 / (4 * H), lam0 / (4 * L)] * 8 + [0.0]
nk = np.array(n, dtype=complex)[:, None] * np.ones_like(wl)

mirror = ScatterMatrix(nk, d, wavelengths=wl, angles=[0.0, 45.0])
out = mirror.compute(Request.RS | Request.RP)  # arrays of shape (angles, wavelengths)
print(f"peak Rs at normal incidence: {out['Rs'][0].max():.4f}")
peak Rs at normal incidence: 0.9987
